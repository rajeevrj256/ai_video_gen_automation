# Sticky reference analysis

The 7 reference videos in `Animated example sticky/` (from channels like Monk Mode Films and Day One Films:
20k–72k subscribers, 15k–913k views a video), studied one by one: every cut, a labelled frame every ~2 s
(~35 contact sheets), consecutive frames of motion, a spectrogram + loudness + onset map of every soundtrack,
AudioSet sound tags per second, and whisper on the voice-like moments.

| Video | Length | Problem | Device that carries the story |
|---|---|---|---|
| DOPAMINE | 2:27 | phone addiction vs a little sister | a battery HUD of *his* energy: 64% → 3% → 64%; clock times; sister's crayon drawing |
| FOCUS | 2:43 | screen time, "start tomorrow" | SCREEN TIME bar + DAY counter + clock; red shadow with notification pills |
| LUST PART 2 | 3:31 | relapse after a long streak | DAY counter 412 → 0; calendar of checks; the voice becomes a copy of him |
| LUST VS LOVE 3 | 3:44 | two strangers, loneliness, debt | split screen, twin counters SEEN 4,206 / KNOWN 0 → 1 |
| LUST | 3:19 | the urge loop | DAY counter; drawn graphs (craving / relief / regret, "an urge is a wave") |
| READY | 2:40 | waiting to feel ready (guitar, open mic) | READY bar stuck at 99% → REPS counter |
| Stop Overthinking | 4:06 | texting a friend, café, class, posting | red sticky notes that pile up on the wall |

## What they are made of

**Characters.** One hero, 0–3 others. A bean/capsule body with a round head (cream `#EFE8DA`-ish fill, thin light
outline), rubber-hose tube limbs with rounded ends (no fingers except on close-up hands), no clothes except one
identity item (hoodie hood, headband, beanie, scarf, bob haircut, cap) that marks who is who and, when it appears
mid-story (the headband in FOCUS, the hood in LUST), marks a change in him. Background people are the same bean
in grey. Faces are two dot or line eyes, eyebrows and a small mouth: the eyebrows carry most emotion (flat =
numb, slanted = determined, raised = worried); tears, sweat drops, dark eye bags and a blush appear only on close-ups.

**The antagonist is a character.** The temptation/fear/overthinking is a black silhouette with a glowing outline
(pink for lust/dopamine, red for fear/anger), crescent eyes and a crescent smile. It whispers in handwritten
coloured text next to it ("just one more.", "you earned it.", "start tomorrow", "they'll all see you fail"), grows
when he gives in, appears as notification pills or sticky notes, can take his own face (LUST PART 2: "It learned
to sound like him"), and dissolves into particles when he walks away. In FOCUS, Stop Overthinking and READY its lines are also whispered (whisper on
those moments: "Just one more.", "You can't stop. You never could.", "Don't mess it up."); the other four stay silent.

**Backgrounds.** Dark (near-black to navy) line-art rooms with only the pieces the story needs: bed, nightstand
with a red digital clock, window with a moon, desk with lamp, door with light behind it, couch, mirror, fridge.
Light does the mood: a cold moonlight beam, a warm lamp pool, a phone glow on the face, colour temperature that
warms as he gets better and drains to grey at the low point. A few richer illustrated sets (city street at dusk,
gym, café, classroom, bus stop in rain, rooftop at sunrise, mountain at sunset) for the turning points.

**Shots.** Wide room shots most of the time, close-up face for a feeling, inserts for objects that carry story:
the phone (chat threads, feeds, lock screen time, a delete dialog), the alarm clock (time = story), the calendar
(checks, a pink scribble on the relapse day), a note, a drawing. Hands on a phone are drawn big. Split screen for
two lives. Graphs drawn into the scene to explain an idea.

**Motion.** Puppet animation at 30 fps: whole-body pivots, walk and run cycles, a long arm reaching, curling up,
shaking with withdrawal, push-ups with a counter, punching through the antagonist's words. The camera is still;
the picture changes every 2–4 s (a new staging, an insert, a time jump) mostly through fades/dissolves, so hard
cuts are rare (2–41 a video) but nothing stays on screen long.

**Words.** No narrator voice (the only voice is the inner voice's whisper in three of them, and a wordless
mumble under a mirror rehearsal). Narration is short on-screen text, centred top or bottom, one or two lines, white,
a rounded sans; the second line often the twist in bold ("He had seen a thousand women. / She used to be on a
screen."). Around 15–30 lines in a whole video. Dialogue is short speech bubbles or chat messages
("were you okay, mira?" / "no."). The antagonist's lines are handwritten in its colour.

**Sound** (every soundtrack is music for 91–100% of the time, mastered around -15 to -20 LUFS):
- a soft music bed (lo-fi piano/plucks, warm pads) that changes with the act: sparse and tense in the spiral, a
  gap of near silence before the turn, brighter in the comeback montage, a tender piano at the end;
- the world's own sounds on every action: phone ding and notification tones, typing, the alarm clock, a knock,
  footsteps, a door, rain, coins, a pen ticking a calendar (AudioSet: Ding, Ringtone, Beep, Knock, Clang, Plop);
- emotion stingers: a heartbeat at the realisation, a chime/bell when something good lands, a rising tone under the
  temptation, deep booms and rumbles when the antagonist appears or wins, static/glitch on a relapse;
- a pop with every speech bubble and handwritten line.
Onsets run 34–104 a minute: something sounds at least every second or two.

## Story shape (all 7)

1. **Cold open text on black** (2 lines, a fact then its twist): "His phone was always at 100%. / He never was."
2. **The loop**, shown not told: the same night repeated with the clock and the counter moving, each time a bit
   worse; the antagonist's lines get more persuasive.
3. **The low point**: grey, small, alone; one line names it ("So he stopped trying.").
4. **The turn from outside him**: a sister's drawing with a phone for his face, a missed call, a friend's text, a
   kid watching through a window. Never a lecture.
5. **DAY 1.** in big chalk letters, then a **montage** of days with the HUD climbing and small concrete habits.
6. **The test**: the antagonist returns smarter ("you've learned your lesson. one look won't hurt."); he wins, or
   falls and recovers ("Twenty two nights won. One lost.").
7. **Callback** to the opening image, now changed ("This time, she drew his face.").
8. **Thesis** in two lines on black ("On day one, it shouts. / On day four hundred, it sounds like you."), then the
   **title card**: one word with a coloured underline, and a two-line tagline whose second line is a dare in the
   accent colour ("Tomorrow is day one. / Or let it win twice."); series add "Subscribe for Part 4."

## Why they work without voice-over or an obvious hook

- **Universal, private problems**: what viewers do alone at 2 AM (scrolling, relapsing, overthinking a text) and
  never say aloud. Seeing it drawn feels like being understood; that is the share and the comment.
- **A number you can watch**: battery %, DAY n, READY 99%, SEEN/KNOWN. The viewer keeps watching to see it fall
  or rise. It is the hook, the progress bar and the score at once.
- **The enemy is visible**: an inner voice drawn as a character makes an abstract struggle a fight you can see.
- **Specific details**: 3:17 AM, "day 30 bro", "i relapsed.", "you too." after "enjoy your meal". Specific beats
  generic every time.
- **Text, not voice**: it reads in any language, works muted, feels like a thought, and each line lands with a
  sound. Short lines leave space for the music.
- **Hope with a cost**: failure is shown honestly, recovery is small and daily; the ending is earned.
- **A title that is a feeling** (READY, DOPAMINE), thumbnails of a sad face and the tempting thing, and a dare in
  the last line.

## Can we make this?

Yes, all of it in code: the bean rig and faces (SVG with Bézier limbs), the shadow with glow and particles, the
line-art rooms and light, phone/chat/calendar/clock inserts, HUD meters, text and bubbles, fades and grey grades,
and the whole sound design from synthesised effects plus a composed or AI music bed per act. What needs Claude is
what made these succeed: a true-to-life problem, the device (meter + antagonist + outside turn), and specific
details. The new pipeline (`reelgen/sticky/`) is built around those, separately from the Stick pipeline.
